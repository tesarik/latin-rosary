# Latin rosary

A small, offline-friendly web app for praying the rosary and other prayers in Latin. The interface is in Czech or English; every prayer is in ecclesiastical Latin, with a Czech or English translation one tap away — instead of the Latin, or under it, line by line.

## Features

**Prayers**

- The rosary in all three traditional mystery sets: **Mysteria Gaudiosa**, **Mysteria Dolorosa**, **Mysteria Gloriosa**. The opening Hail Marys carry the `qui adaúgeat nobis fidem` / `corróboret nobis spem` / `perfíciat nobis caritátem` clauses, each decade's mystery clause is set into its Hail Marys, and the Fatima prayer (`O mi Iesu`) follows each Gloria Patri.
- The other prayers are grouped by occasion: **daily prayers** (the everyday prayers, both Creeds and the Athanasian Creed, the Angelus, the *Te Deum*, grace before and after meals, Ánima Christi, St. Ignatius's *Súscipe*, St. Thomas Aquinas's prayer before study, *Nunc dimíttis* for the night, the *Miserére* (Psalm 50), and more), **to Our Lady** (the four seasonal antiphons, Sub tuum præsídium, the *Memoráre*, the *Magníficat*, Stabat Mater, the hymn *Ave, maris stella*), **the Mass**, **to the Blessed Sacrament** (*Adóro te devóte*, *Ave verum*, *O salutáris Hóstia*, *O sacrum convívium*, *Panis angélicus*, *Pange lingua* and *Tantum ergo* with the versicle and collect before Benediction), **litanies**, **for the dead** and **devotions** (the chaplets of the Holy Spirit, the Precious Blood, the Sacred Heart and St. Michael, the Rosary of the Seven Sorrows, and the prayers to St. Joseph: *Ad te, beáte Ioseph*, his *Memoráre*, St. Bernardine's *Meménto nostri*, *O Ioseph, virgo Pater Iesu*, the invocation *Fac nos innócuam* and his commemoration).
- **For the dead**: Réquiem, *De profúndis* (Psalm 129 with its versicles and collect), *Dies iræ* and the *Preces pro defunctis*.
- **Ordo Missæ**: the people's parts of the traditional Latin Mass, in the order of the Mass: Psalm 42 *Iúdica me* at the foot of the altar, *Confíteor* (with the *Misereátur* and *Indulgéntiam*), *Kýrie*, *Glória in excélsis*, the dialogue before the Preface (*Sursum corda*), *Sanctus*, *Agnus Dei*, the priest's prayers before Communion, *Dómine, non sum dignus* and the priest's prayers after Communion. Each can be opened on its own, or the whole *Ordo Missæ* (with the *Credo*) stepped through in order at Mass, like the rosary, keeping its place.
- **Sequéntiæ**, with the Mass: *Víctimæ pascháli laudes* (Easter), *Veni, Sancte Spíritus* (Pentecost) and *Lauda Sion* (Corpus Christi); *Stabat Mater* and *Dies iræ* stand with Our Lady and the dead. After the Mass, the Leonine prayers.
- **Seven litanies**: Loreto, the Sacred Heart, the Holy Name, St. Joseph, the Precious Blood, All Saints (traditional form) and the Litany of Humility.
- The **Chaplet of Divine Mercy** (*Corona Divínæ Misericórdiæ*), prayed on the same ring of beads as the rosary. A switch on its tile adds the optional prayers from St. Faustina's Diary.
- The **Leonine prayers** (after Low Mass), the **prayers of St. Bridget**, the **Seven Sorrows and Joys of St. Joseph** in its short Latin form, and the **prayers for the dead** (*Preces pro defunctis*: Pater Noster, Ave María, *De profúndis*, its versicles and collect, step by step).
- The start screen has **three tabs** at the bottom: *Rosary* (the mysteries, today's first, and the Chaplet of Divine Mercy), *Prayers* (search, the *In season* card and the groups) and *Mine* (favourites, prayer plans and settings). **Help** and **About** are at the foot of every tab.
- **Favourites**: star any prayer, litany or devotion from its header (☆), and it appears in a *Favourites* card under *Mine*, beside the prayer plans. Kept in the browser, like the plans.
- Three looks, each light or dark: **Základ** (the default), in cool greys; **Papír**, set like a missal — Latin names in EB Garamond, warm paper tones, each prayer opening with a red initial; and **Misál**, a white page in black and red only, with woodcuts from Roman missals of the 1880s heading the prayer groups, the *Moje* tab, the plans, Help and About, and a red tailpiece closing each prayer (a thorn-crowned cross after the Passion, the sorrows and the prayers for the dead). Chosen in the ⋯ settings or the *Moje* tab.
- The light/dark toggle and the language (a flag) sit in the top corners of every start-screen tab.
- **Search** the prayers by their Latin, Czech or English name, accents optional (*memorare*, *pomni*, *credo*).
- Today's mysteries lead the start screen as its largest tile.
- On a first launch, a short **welcome** card says where to start, how to switch between Latin and the translation, and where the other prayers are.
- While praying, the *Next* button names the next prayer, and a litany or devotion shows its step (*5 of 11*). Text size, dark mode, a **dimmed screen** for church and the **vibration** on each step are under the header's ⋯ button.
- Coming back from a prayer, the start screen is where you left it: on the same tab, scrolled to the same place, with the same groups open.
- The other prayers are grouped by occasion, each group in alphabetical order of the Latin names; the Mass keeps the order of the Mass.
- **Prayer plans**: build your own named sequence of prayers, litanies, the Leonine prayers, the prayers for the dead and **prayers of your own** (Latin text plus an optional translation), with repeats. Share a plan as a link. Plans are stored only in the browser.
- **Backup**: plans, favourites, unfinished prayers and settings saved to a file under *Mine*, and loaded back — on a new phone, say. Loading adds and never deletes.
- **Pronouncing the Latin**: a short guide to the Roman pronunciation in Help.
- The start screen marks **today's mysteries**, by weekday and on Sunday by liturgical season, and gathers **what the day calls for** in an *In season* card (closed until tapped): the Marian antiphon of the season, the sequence in the octaves of Easter and Pentecost, in Corpus Christi week and on the feasts of Our Lady of Sorrows, the Litany of the Saints on All Saints, the prayers for the dead through November, the litany of the month's devotion (St. Joseph in March, the Sacred Heart in June and on first Fridays, the Precious Blood in July, the Holy Name in January, Loreto in May and October) with the chaplet of the Sacred Heart or the Precious Blood, the Rosary of the Seven Sorrows on Our Lady of Sorrows and in Lent, and the St. Bridget prayers in Lent.

**Praying**

- An SVG rosary: 5 Pater Noster + 50 Ave Maria beads on the ring, and a tail with the Creed, Pater Noster, 3 Hail Marys and the cross. The active bead glows in the mystery's accent colour. Tap a Pater Noster bead to jump to that decade. The Chaplet of Divine Mercy uses the same ring.
- Litanies and the other multi-step prayers step through their **sections** on a bead strand. In a litany, a response shared by several invocations is shown **once**, in a band that stays at the top of the card (*℟ ora pro nobis*), instead of on every line.
- **Unfinished prayers keep their place**: leave the rosary to pray something else, and come back to *Pokračovat · 23/74*. The last unfinished prayer is offered at the top of the start screen, with a × to forget it. Every multi-step prayer remembers its own step across reloads.
- Tap or swipe to move on or back. On a keyboard, `→` / `Space` / `Enter` move on and `←` goes back. Every step gives a haptic tick.
- A first-time user gets three short hints in the prayer, one at a time and once each: how to step, that a larger bead jumps to another decade or part, and that `←` keeps the place.
- The header holds the controls: back and restart, the favourites star, the ⋯ settings (look, text size, dark mode, dimmed screen, vibration), and a three-way switch for the prayer language: Latin, Latin with the translation under each line, or the translation (Czech/English).
- In the rosary, the label names the current decade and quotes its mystery in full, in the language the prayer is shown in (*II. quem, Virgo, visitándo Elisabeth portásti* / *2. desátek: s kterým jsi, Panno, Alžbětu navštívila*), and a double vibration marks the end of each decade (in the chaplet too).
- Turn a phone sideways, or use a tablet, and the beads and the prayer sit side by side.
- **Wake Lock** keeps the screen on while praying.

**App**

- Czech and English interface, picked from the browser and switchable on the start screen.
- Light and dark themes; follows the system until you choose.
- **PWA**: installable, works offline, and offers an in-app update when a new version is deployed.
- Prayer text set in **EB Garamond**, bundled with the app so it works offline and nothing is loaded from Google; UI in Arial.
- Screen-reader friendly: ARIA labels on controls and a live region around the prayer text.

**On the web**

- **Every prayer has its own page**, in English and Czech: the Latin beside the translation, generated from the app's own texts, so search engines can find them. A button on each page opens the prayer straight in the app (`#pray=<key>`). *About* links to the index.
- **Link previews**: a shared link shows the Immaculate Heart, the app's icon, with *Rosarium Latinum* and a short description.

## Run locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Then open the URL Vite prints (default `http://localhost:5173`).

## Build

```bash
npm run build       # typecheck, then build into ./dist
npm run preview     # serves ./dist locally
```

Other scripts: `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm test` (Vitest).

### Deploying under a sub-path

If the app lives at `https://example.com/latin-rosary/`, set `BASE_PATH` at build time:

```bash
BASE_PATH=/latin-rosary/ npm run build
```

Set `SITE_URL` (the origin, e.g. `SITE_URL=https://example.com`) as well, so link previews and the prayer pages get absolute URLs and the build writes `sitemap.xml`; the build warns when it is missing. The manifest, icons and service-worker scope all derive from `BASE_PATH`. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` (or `.env.production`) to keep it set locally. Every build gets its own service-worker cache, so installed copies are offered the update.

### Prayer pages for search engines

The build also writes a static page for every prayer, in English and Czech (`en/…`, `cs/…`, with an index at `en/` and `cs/`): the Latin beside the translation, with a link that opens the prayer in the app. With `SITE_URL` set it writes `sitemap.xml` too. Under a sub-path, `robots.txt` has no effect, so submit the sitemap (e.g. `https://example.com/latin-rosary/sitemap.xml`) in Google Search Console.

### After deploying

1. Open the app and a prayer page or two (`…/en/`, `…/cs/`), also on a phone: the pages switch to one column below 720 px.
2. If prayer pages were added or renamed, submit or resubmit the sitemap in Google Search Console.
3. To see or refresh a link preview, paste the URL into the [Facebook Sharing Debugger](https://developers.facebook.com/tools/debug/).

### Releasing

On the PR branch, commit `chore: release X.Y.Z`: `npm version X.Y.Z --no-git-tag-version`, rename `[Unreleased]` to `[X.Y.Z]` in `CHANGELOG.md`, and update the example version in `CLAUDE.md`. Merge the PR, then `gh release create X.Y.Z --target main` with the changelog section as the notes.

## Project layout

- `src/Rosary.tsx`: the orchestrator. It holds the state and the saved progress, and lays out the prayer screen.
- `src/rosary/`:
  - Prayer data (`prayers.ts`) and sequences (`sequence.ts`), litany parsing (`litany.ts`), navigation and persistence logic (`navigation.ts`, `storage.ts`), the chaplet's optional-prayers switch (`chaplet.ts`), plans (`plans.ts`), and the static prayer pages and sitemap (`staticPages.ts`).
  - The components: `MysteryMenu`, `PrayerCard`, `RosaryBeads`, `PrayerSections`, `PlansScreen`, and the header controls.
  - Unit tests: `*.test.ts`. Component tests (`src/Rosary.test.tsx`, React Testing Library in jsdom) drive the start screen and the prayer screen as a user would.
- `src/main.tsx`: the entry point, with an error boundary around the app.
- `public/icons/*.webp`: the paintings on the start screen's mystery-set and chaplet tiles, rendered from `art/menu-icons/` by `python3 scripts/render-menu-icons.py`.
- `public/missal/*.webp`: the Misál look's woodcuts (public domain, from 1880s Roman missals), rendered from `art/missal/` by `python3 scripts/render-missal-art.py`.
- `public/manifest.webmanifest`, `public/sw.js` and the icons `public/icon-48/180/192/512.png` and `public/icon-maskable-192/512.png`: PWA assets. The icons are rendered from `art/icon-source.png` by `python3 scripts/render-icons.py` (needs Pillow and NumPy), which also renders `public/heart-192.webp`, the heart shown in the app on a transparent background, from `art/icon-heart.png` (cut out of the blue field by `python3 scripts/cut-heart.py`).
- `public/og-image.svg`: the link-preview image, rendered to `og-image.png` (1200×630) the same way; EB Garamond must be installed.
- `index.html`: title, description, link-preview tags, theme colour, and the pre-paint theme script.
- `vite.config.ts`: reads `BASE_PATH` and `SITE_URL`, stamps the version, build date and SW cache version, fills in the link-preview URLs, and writes the prayer pages and sitemap.
- `CHANGELOG.md`: release notes (Keep a Changelog format).
- `CLAUDE.md`: detailed orientation notes for AI-assisted development.

## Stack

React 18, Vite 6, TypeScript (strict mode), Vitest. No CSS framework, no router, no state library. Styling is inline, with light/dark colour tokens in `src/index.css`.

## Sources

The Latin and most of the Czech prayer texts are transcribed from `Posvátný růženec latinsko – česky.pdf` (not included in the repo) and other printed and liturgical sources; the Czech *Ordo Missæ*, sequences, prayers for the dead and Litany of the Saints come from Schaller's *Římský misál* (1931) and the Czech burial rite (*Manuale rituum*, 1955); the Czech everyday prayers, *Memoráre*, *Magníficat*, *Nunc dimíttis* and the prayers to St. Joseph from the prayer books *Příručka katolických pobožností* (1935), *Katechismus modliteb* (1937) and *Cesta k věčné spáse* (1915). The English texts follow the received traditional English forms and were checked against published witnesses; the English *Ordo Missæ* and sequences are from the *Saint Andrew Daily Missal*. The Czech *Te Deum* is Dr. Hejčl's prose (*Příručka katolických pobožností*, 1935), the hymns to the Blessed Sacrament the versions in that book, in *Katechismus modliteb* and *Cesta k věčné spáse*, and the Czech *Ave verum* a modern translation by Lenka Jeřábková (Collegium Strahoviense). The Czech *Ave, maris stella* is from *Cesta k věčné spáse* ([1932]), *O sacrum convívium* from *Modleme se!* (1916), *Panis angélicus* from *Posvátné zpěvy a modlitby* (1888) and the *Miserére* from *Malé hodinky k nejblahoslavenější Panně Marii* (1937), all found in the Czech National Library's digital library (Kramerius). A few texts have no authoritative original and are our own, such as the St. Bridget prayers, the Latin of the chaplet's optional prayers, the Czech and English headings of St. Joseph's sorrows and joys, the Czech of St. Thomas Aquinas's prayer before study, the English *Ave verum* and *Panis angélicus*, the Latin of St. Joseph's *Memoráre* (an Italian prayer that the *Raccolta* prints only in English), the Veni Creator in the English Chaplet of the Holy Spirit (the rest of it is the *Raccolta*'s), and the Latin of the chaplets of St. Michael, the Seven Sorrows, the Precious Blood and the Sacred Heart (Raccolta devotions with no Latin edition; their Czech is from *Cesta k věčné spáse* and *Poutní kniha* (1893), their English from the *Raccolta*). `src/rosary/prayers.ts` records where each text comes from.

The paintings on the mystery-set and chaplet tiles and the Immaculate Heart of the app icon were made with the help of AI. The woodcuts of the *Misál* look are engravings from a Roman Missal whose plates are dated 1883–1887 (public domain).
